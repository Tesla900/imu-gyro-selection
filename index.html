Which gyroscope? Nine IMUs ranked for drones, robots and navigation

Nine 6-axis MEMS IMUs compared on gyro noise, bias instability, temperature stability, g-sensitivity, range, bandwidth and power. Vendor conventions are put on a common basis first. Whatever a datasheet does not state is kept as unknown and shown as a range, never as a guess.

Section 3Putting the numbers on one basis

Seven conventions differ between these datasheets. Each is either converted or flagged; none is compared raw.

IssueWhat the datasheets doWhat this report does
ARW conventionMurata defines its printed ARW as the Allan deviation at τ = 1 s (÷ 60), and the Analog Devices values match the same quantity in its own plots. The other six print only a noise density. The common conversion ARW = ND × 60 puts those on a different basis.A printed noise density is one-sided, so σ(1 s) = ND / √2 and ARW = ND × 60 / √2. The three datasheets that print both confirm it: their ND × 60 is 1.3–1.6 times the printed ARW.
Bias-instability definitionAnalog Devices: minimum of the Allan curve. Murata: that minimum ÷ 0.664. Nobody else specifies it.Compare raw Allan minima (Murata values × 0.664).
What “typ” meansADIS16607: “1σ”, which equals the worst-axis mean + σ curve of its own plots. SCH16T: population mean, limits ±3σ. BMI088: limits ±3σ. TDK, ST, BMI563: not defined.±3σ limits are divided by 3 to get a 1σ-equivalent. Undefined “typ” values are used as printed and marked B. ADIS16607 values are used as printed and noted as conservative; its plot means are shown next to them.
Temperature: slope or bandADIS16607 and SCH16T give a total error band over a range. The others give a typical slope, some over a partial or unstated range.Express both as a typical-equivalent change across −40…+85 °C: band as printed (1σ) or ÷ 3 (±3σ); slope × 65 K. Extrapolated slopes are marked C.
g-sensitivity definitionSCH16T: static gravity, ±3σ. ADIS16607: any direction, 1σ. BMI088: dynamic stimulus up to 20 kHz, maximum.Convert to a 1σ-equivalent and score it, but say plainly that the three tests are not the same.
Supply currentQuoted at 3.3 V, 3.0 V or 1.8 V, in differently named modes. BMI563 states no supply; BMI088 states no combined current.Compare power = current × test voltage. BMI563 is assumed at 1.8 V; BMI088 uses the sum of its two typical currents.
Flyer and short-form gapsBMI563 is a preliminary flyer (32 of the 53 parameters missing). The SCH16T short forms omit the register map, filter defaults and sleep current.Unknown stays unknown. Every BMI563 value is marked C.
Effect on the ranking. One position changes once ARW is on a single basis: converted as ND × 60, ICM-45686 comes out at 0.228 °/√h and appears worse than ADIS16607-2 (0.19 °/√h printed). On the Allan basis it is 0.161 °/√h and ranks ahead. Every comparison between one of the six parts without a printed ARW and one of the three with it shifts by the same factor of √2.

4.3Stability over temperature

4.5Range

Seven candidates reach ±2000 °/s or more. ADIS16607-2 and SCH16T-K20 trade range for noise: ±480 and ±300 °/s. The ±4000 °/s setting of LSM6DSV has no noise or sensitivity specification. SCH16T-K10 keeps an unsaturated output up to ±5243 °/s at default sensitivity, which helps during crashes and impacts.

Every metric is mapped to 0–10 on a log scale between two fixed anchors: score = 10 × log(worst / value) / log(worst / best), clipped to 0–10. 6.2The main trade-offs

  • Noise against range. SCH16T-K20 gets its noise by limiting itself to ±300 °/s. Within one family the step is visible too: ADIS16607-3 gives 4× the range of the -2 for 1.3× the ARW and 3.3× the printed bias instability.
  • Evidence against power and size. The parts whose drift you can read off the datasheet draw 50–135 mW and occupy 48–158 mm². The parts that fit in 7.5 mm² at about 1 mW leave bias instability and g-sensitivity for you to measure.
  • Latency against filtering. ADIS16607 (0.24 ms) and ICM-42688-P (0.1–0.2 ms) suit kHz control loops. SCH16T filtered outputs add 1–2 ms at 370 Hz.
  • Typical against specified. A “±0.005 °/s/°C typ” and a “±0.3 °/s ±3σ over −40…+110 °C” can describe similar hardware. Only the second lets you write an error budget.
  • Temperature ceiling. SCH16T runs to +110 °C and ADIS16607 to +105 °C (calibrated to +85 °C); the rest stop at +85 °C. SCH16T-K20 loses its X/Y advantage above 85 °C.
  • Linearity at high rate. ADIS16607-3 allows 0.6 % of full scale (12 °/s at ±2000 °/s, 1σ, against a fit over ±400 °/s). SCH16T-K10 states ±1.5 °/s at ±3σ; BMI088 and the two ICM parts state ±0.05–0.1 % typical against a best-fit line; LSM6DSV and BMI563 state nothing. The definitions differ, but the order of magnitude matters for fast flips and spins.
  • Accelerometer headroom. If shocks or strong vibration are expected, the ±40 g of ADIS16607 and the ±24–32 g of BMI088, ICM-45686, LSM6DSV32X and BMI563 clip later than ±16 g or the ±8 g of SCH16T-K20.

6.3What to watch out for and ask the vendor

Analog Devices · ADIS16607

  • Which in-run bias stability applies to the -3: 7.5 °/h (Table 1) or at most 5.8 °/h (Fig. 7)?
  • How is “1σ” defined, and how many devices are behind the figures? The ±3σ envelopes in the plots are about the size of the 1σ table limits, not three times larger.
  • Vibration error against level and spectrum: Fig. 15 is roughly flat from 4 to 16 g rms, so the per-g² figure cannot be scaled.
  • Bias repeatability is 0.64 °/s (1σ), two to three times the temperature error. How much of it is turn-on drift that a start-up zero removes?
  • The sample clock varies from 8 to 11 kHz between parts, and filter corners move with it. Plan on the sync input.
  • No sleep mode or sleep current; start-up 130 ms in the table, 200 ms wait in the initialization sequence.

Murata · SCH16T-K10 / K20

  • Get the full datasheets (62 and 67 pages): register map, default filter, low-power mode, self-test, sensitivity settings.
  • g-sensitivity is static gravity only. Ask for vibration rectification or random-vibration data; none is printed.
  • K20: X/Y offset reaches ±0.7 °/s at 110 °C and the +3σ curve ends at the top of the plot, still rising. Ask for data above 85 °C.
  • K10: no gyro Allan plot and no nominal group delay. K10 bypass bandwidth and delay are not in the short form.
  • Every value is “not guaranteed” (population statistics). Ask for sample sizes and for guaranteed limits if you need them.
  • Specifications apply 24 hours after reflow; ultrasonic cleaning is prohibited; 41 mA is given without a named mode.

TDK InvenSense · ICM-42688-P / ICM-45686

  • Bias instability or Allan data, g-sensitivity and vibration error: none appears in either datasheet.
  • ICM-42688-P: gyro offset and sensitivity slopes are stated for 0…+70 °C only. Ask for −40…+85 °C.
  • ICM-42688-P: group delay of the anti-alias and notch filters, which is not included in the 0.1–0.2 ms.
  • ICM-45686: filter group delay and anti-alias response (the datasheet defers both to an application note), any errata, and offsets after soldering (the datasheet gives component-level values).
  • What statistic is “typ”? Temperature slopes, nonlinearity and cross-axis values come from characterization and are not production tested.

STMicroelectronics · LSM6DSV16X / 32X

  • Bias instability, g-sensitivity, nonlinearity and cross-axis sensitivity are all absent.
  • The offset slope has no temperature range and comes from “a limited number of samples”.
  • The ±1 °/s zero-rate level is a “value after calibration”. Which calibration, and does it hold after soldering?
  • Group delay of the main (UI) path; noise and sensitivity at ±4000 °/s (specified to ±2000 °/s only).
  • Output-rate accuracy outside the high-accuracy ODR mode is not specified.

Bosch Sensortec · BMI088 / BMI563

  • BMI088: bias instability, group delay and resonance frequency; whether the widest bandwidth is 523 or 532 Hz.
  • BMI088: how the 0.1 (°/s)/g limit is tested and what a typical value is, since competitors quote typical or σ values.
  • BMI563: everything. A datasheet with test conditions, typical noise instead of “<” bounds, the temperature range of the slopes, bandwidth and delay.
  • BMI563: the numbers behind “vibration robustness from 0 to 40 kHz”, the supply and mode of the 0.65 mA figure, and availability.

For any of them, before committing

  • Measure Allan deviation on three to five samples at your filter setting and temperature. It takes a night and settles the bias-instability question for the LGA parts.
  • Run a temperature sweep on the assembled board. Soldering stress shifts offsets; several datasheets quote component-level values.
  • Check driver support in your flight stack or robot middleware, plus price, lead time and an evaluation board. None of this is in the datasheets and it often decides.
  • On drones, plan soft mounting and check the gyro spectrum in flight before trusting any noise figure.

Section 8Consistency checks, open points and limits

How the numbers were cross-checked, where the datasheets contradict themselves, and how far the conclusions carry.

Consistency checks

  • Curves against tables. The Allan deviation at τ = 1 s and the curve minima computed from the digitized plots agree with the printed ARW and bias-instability values (table in section 4.2). For ADIS16607-2 the printed values equal the worst-axis mean + σ curve. For SCH16T-K20 the medians lie at or just above the printed typical values and below the printed maxima. The one exception, ADIS16607-3, is listed below.
  • Values that look like misprints. Two were read again in the datasheets and are printed as used here: the SCH16T-K20 X/Y offset of ±0.7 °/s (Table 4, p. 6) and the ADIS16607-3 in-run bias stability of 7.5 °/h (Table 1, p. 5). The BMI088 datasheet was also searched for a bias-stability statement; it has none.
  • Data entry. All 219 datasheet values used in the tables and scores were transcribed from the datasheets and then verified by an automated check against that transcription.

Inconsistencies inside the datasheets

  • ADIS16607-3 bias stability: 7.5 °/h in the table, at most 5.8 °/h in the plot. Unexplained in the datasheet.
  • ADIS16607 plots against table: the ±3σ envelopes of offset over temperature are only 1.3× (-2) and 1.9× (-3) the “1σ” table values, where 3× would be expected. Either the table is conservative or it is defined differently.
  • ADIS16607 vibration error does not scale with g² (about 2–15 °/h from 4 to 16 g rms), although the table quotes it per g².
  • SCH16T-K20 is worse than SCH16T-K10 on X/Y offset above 85 °C (±0.7 against ±0.3 °/s at ±3σ up to 110 °C). The plotted curve ends at the axis limit, so the true value at 110 °C may be higher.
  • BMI088 prints 523 Hz in its specification table and 532 Hz in its register table for the same setting.

Limits of this analysis

  • Datasheet values only. Nothing here is a measurement. Typical values are not guaranteed, and the plots are typical-performance data with unstated sample sizes.
  • The scores depend on the anchors and weights listed in sections 4.8 and 5. They are stated so that you can disagree with them; the panel in section 5 recomputes the ranking for other weights.
  • Counting an unknown as 5 is a convention, not information. Read the range, not only the dot. Vendors rarely leave out a flattering number, but that is a judgement and is not built into the scores.
  • The temperature and g-sensitivity comparisons rest on assumptions (typ ≈ 1σ, ±3σ ÷ 3, linear slope over 65 K, three different g-sensitivity tests). They rank orders of magnitude, not small differences.
  • Not covered: price, availability, software support, long-term supply, and anything in the documents that were not available (the SCH16T full datasheets, a BMI563 datasheet).
